Register-Invariant Jamo Coding for Short Korean Text

J. Suh et al.
ICTC 2026  ·  In preparation, 2026

Compact text encodings assume a small alphabet. However, Korean does not have one, as Hangul (the Korean alphabet) is written as 11,172 precomposed syllable blocks, far beyond the 128 symbols the national language shift tables in 3GPP TS 23.038 can address. Message channels therefore fall back to UCS-2 at sixteen bits per character, which is why an 82-octet Cell Broadcast page holds only 41 Korean characters.

This paper proposes and evaluates M0, a 42B decoder state static entropy code that takes each syllable apart into onset, vowel and coda and codes the three positions with static Huffman tables fitted to public Wikipedia text. On 27,039 authentic Korean emergency alerts M0 is lossless and cuts the mean alert by 37.0% against UCS-2, lifting page capacity from 41 characters to 65. Among codecs whose decoder state fits inside a specification it achieves the lowest mean, and it beats zstd with a trained dictionary on both rate and memory.

Furthermore, refitting M0 on alert text rather than encyclopedia text moves its rate by about 1.3%, while the same refit moves an order-1 character model by 44.4%. This shows that M0’s compression is a property of the Korean language itself rather than of the corpus it was trained on, which proves it is apt for adaptation as a potential standard.
